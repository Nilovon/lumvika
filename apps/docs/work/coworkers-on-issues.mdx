---
title: "Coworkers on issues"
description: "What happens when you assign an issue to a coworker: when it wakes, what it does, and the rules it cannot break."
---

You assign an issue to a coworker the way you assign it to a person. You do not message it or schedule anything: it wakes up on its own, takes the issue, does the work, comments on it and hands it back.

## When a coworker wakes up

| What happens | When it wakes the coworker |
| --- | --- |
| The issue is assigned to it, or created already assigned to it | When the issue is in **Todo**, **In progress** or **Blocked**. Not in **Backlog** |
| The issue moves from **Backlog** to **Todo** | Always |
| Someone moves the issue back to **Todo** or **In progress**: reopened from **Done**, sent back from **In review**, unblocked | Always |
| A person comments on the issue | When it is in **Todo**, **In progress**, **Blocked** or **In review** |
| The heartbeat, every 5 minutes | When the coworker is idle and an issue is waiting on it. See [The heartbeat](#the-heartbeat) |

A coworker is never woken by what it did itself, nor by another coworker's comment, nor while one of its runs is already working on the issue.

<Tip>
  An issue assigned in **Backlog** waits there. Move it to **Todo** when it is ready, and the coworker starts.
</Tip>

## What it does

<Steps>
  <Step title="It reads the issue">
    The description, the project's **Instructions for coworkers**, the goal it serves, its parent and sub-issues, who asked for it, and the latest 50 comments.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It takes the issue">
    The issue moves to **In progress** and is held by that run. The issue page says **Scout is working on this issue**. No other run can take it until this one ends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It does the work">
    With its own tools, skills and apps, as in any other run, following the project's instructions. It comments on what matters as it goes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="It hands the issue back">
    To **In review** with what it did and where to find it, to **Blocked** with who must do what, or to **Done** when nobody needs to check.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each issue gets a conversation of its own in the coworker's history, named after the issue, such as `WEB-5: Research competitor pricing pages`. **Open conversation**, on the issue page, opens it so you can follow every step.

A coworker works one issue at a time. A second issue assigned while it is busy waits in its queue, and the heartbeat hands it over once the coworker is free.

An issue run uses the coworker's default model, like a scheduled task. It can stop to ask you a question or an approval, exactly like a scheduled run: the conversation then waits for you in [Review](/work/review), under **Waiting in conversations**.

## The rules a coworker follows

A coworker works within the same [allowed moves](/work/issues#allowed-moves) as a person, plus these:

- **It moves only the issues assigned to it.** On any other issue it can comment, nothing more.
- **It cannot close an issue a person asked for.** It cannot move an issue created by a person to **Done** or **Cancelled**: it moves it to **In review**, and a person approves it. To suggest dropping one, it moves it to **Blocked** and says why.
- **It can close its own work.** An issue created by a coworker, such as a sub-issue it filed, it can move to **Done** or **Cancelled** itself.
- **Blocked always carries a reason**, naming who must do what: "Anna must share the Stripe API key".
- **Backlog is a person's call.** A coworker hands an issue back with **Todo** or explains with **Blocked**, never parks it.

When a run ends, whatever it held is released. A hold whose run is gone is released by the heartbeat, and no hold lasts more than a day.

## Splitting work

A coworker can split an issue into sub-issues and assign them, to itself or to other coworkers by their handle. A sub-issue it assigns starts in **Todo**, and the coworker it is assigned to is woken. One it does not assign starts in **Backlog**.

It can then search the issues of its projects to follow the work it handed out.

## The heartbeat

Every 5 minutes, each machine checks, for every idle coworker with **Work on issues** switched on, whether an issue waits on it. It hands over at most one issue per coworker, in this order: **In progress** first, then **Todo**, then **Blocked**, each by priority, then by due date.

An issue is handed over when:

- the coworker never worked on it, or
- someone else did something on it since the coworker last did: a comment, a move, a new assignee, or
- the coworker last touched it more than a day ago and it is not blocked.

So a blocked issue waits until someone else acts on it, and an issue the coworker left open is brought back to it once a day. The same issue is not handed to the same coworker twice within an hour.

The heartbeat skips archived projects, switched-off coworkers, and issues a run already holds. Issue runs pause like scheduled runs when the workspace's Lumvika AI budget runs out, for coworkers on Lumvika models.

## When it hands an issue back

When a coworker moves an issue to **In review** or **Blocked**, a notice reaches, in the app:

- the person who created the issue, and
- the person who leads the project.

Someone who muted that coworker in their notification settings gets nothing. There is no email and no push for these notices yet. The issue also shows up in [Review](/work/review) for everyone who can open the project.

## Switch it on or off

**Work on issues** is a capability of every coworker, switched on by default. Find it in the coworker **Capabilities** tab, which appears with **Expert mode**. See [Capabilities](/agents/configure#capabilities).

Switched off, the coworker is never woken for an issue and has no tool to read or move one, even if you ask it in a chat. The issues assigned to it stay where they are. A switched-off coworker (the **Enabled** setting) is not woken either.

## What a coworker can see

A coworker reaches the projects through its own tools, not through your access.

- **Projects open to the workspace**: it sees them all, with every issue.
- **Restricted projects**: it sees one only when it leads the project, holds one of its issues or created one. It then sees every issue of that project.
- Any other restricted project does not exist for it.

<Warning>
  Anyone who can talk to a coworker can ask it what it read. Before you assign an issue of a restricted project to a coworker, make sure the people who reach that coworker's Box may know about the whole project.
</Warning>

The conversation of each issue run sits in the coworker's history like any of its conversations, so everyone who can open the coworker can read it.

## Older machines

A machine running a version of Lumvika from before issues does not wake its coworkers: you can assign them issues, and nothing happens. On a self-hosted install, [update the engine](/self-host/servers#updating).
